Downtown Duluth around the Town Green

Downtown Duluth is the city's historic center. The city's 2014 downtown master plan describes it as centered on the Town Green, which holds City Hall and other public buildings. City Hall is at 3167 Main Street.

The plan's study area covers about 76 acres. It runs from the railroad on the south to the north side of Hill Street, with Brock Road at the eastern edge and single-family subdivisions on the west.

Main Street holds the older storefronts. The plan says fewer than a dozen commercial buildings predate 1940, and about half of the historic Main Street buildings date to roughly 1880 and half to about 1910. The city's 2008 plan adds that historic homes ring the downtown, with the largest groups on Lawrenceville Street and Main Street.

What this mix means for plumbing calls

A downtown block can hold a restaurant, an office and a home within a short walk, and each one needs different things from its plumbing. Food service needs floor drains and grease handling. Shops and offices need restrooms and hot water that work during business hours. Homes need the same repairs as anywhere else in Duluth.

Tell us which kind of building you have when you call. For a business, our commercial plumbing page explains the work, and for a home the drain cleaning and water heater repair pages cover the usual calls.

Plumbing points for older downtown buildings

Treat these as general guidance. They are not a claim about any particular building.

Check the pipe before you plan

Older buildings have often been remodeled more than once, so the pipe in a wall may be a mix of materials. A plumber should look before quoting a repair.

Find the shutoffs early

Locate the main shutoff and any floor drains before a problem starts, especially in a building with more than one tenant.

Keep grease out of the line

Kitchens send grease into drains, and it builds up fast. Ask Gwinnett County Water Resources what rules apply to a food business.

Ask about backflow devices

Some commercial properties need backflow devices tested on a schedule. Ask the water utility or the City of Duluth what applies to your building.

Use a camera on repeat clogs

A line that keeps backing up deserves a sewer camera inspection so you see the cause before you pay for a fix.

Preservation districts and permits

The city's 2008 plan describes two Core Preservation Districts, one residential and one commercial, each with its own review board. If your building might be inside one, ask the City of Duluth Planning and Development team about review before you change the outside of a building.

Plumbing permits are a separate question, so ask the city what applies to your address. City Hall at 3167 Main Street handles city permits and services.

Water, sewer and stormwater downtown

Gwinnett County Water Resources provides water and sewer in Duluth. The 2014 plan also says downtown was underserved by existing stormwater facilities and sits in two drainage basins.

Public drainage is a city matter and separate from the pipes inside your building. Still, a floor drain can back up when the public system is overloaded, and it can also back up because of a clog in your own line. We help you work out whether the problem is inside the building or outside it, and we say so plainly.

What we verified and what we do not know

Verified from public sources: the Town Green and City Hall location, the 76 acre study area, the ages of the historic Main Street buildings and the Core Preservation Districts, all from city plans.

General plumbing information: grease, backflow and cleanout guidance for older and mixed use buildings.

Unknown: what is inside the walls of any single building and whether your address sits inside a preservation district. Ask the city for the second and a plumber for the first.
