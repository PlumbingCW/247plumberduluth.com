How to Flush a Water Heater in Duluth, GA

Sediment settles at the bottom of every tank, and over time it lowers efficiency and shortens the tank's life. A yearly flush is a simple job that most homeowners can do with a garden hose.

Why a flush helps

Water carries dissolved minerals, and the heat of the tank makes some of them drop out and settle at the bottom as sediment. A layer of sediment insulates the water from the burner or the lower element, which wastes energy and can cause popping and rumbling noises.

Sediment also feeds corrosion and clogs the drain valve. The U.S. Department of Energy recommends draining a quart or so every three months, or flushing the tank once a year, to remove it. A tank that has never been flushed may have a thick layer that is hard to remove.

What you need

Gather a few items before you start.

  • 1A garden hose long enough to reach a floor drain or the yard.
  • 2A bucket or two for the first water, which may be dirty.
  • 3A flat screwdriver or a wrench for the drain valve.
  • 4Work gloves, and old towels in case of drips.
  • 5The owner's manual, in case your model has special steps.

The flushing steps

The steps are almost the same for gas and electric tanks.

  • 1Turn off the power or gas. For electric tanks, switch off the breaker. For gas tanks, set the control to pilot or off.
  • 2Close the cold inlet. Shut the cold water valve on top of the tank.
  • 3Attach the hose. Connect the hose to the drain valve and run the other end to a drain or outdoors, downhill.
  • 4Open a hot tap. Open a hot faucet upstairs to let air in, so the tank can drain.
  • 5Open the drain valve. Let the tank empty. Water will be hot at first, so be careful.
  • 6Flush with cold water. Open the cold inlet briefly to stir the sediment and drain until the water runs clear.
  • 7Close up and refill. Close the drain valve, fill the tank with the hot tap open and restore power or gas only when full.

Problems you may find

A drain valve that will not open or stays clogged with sediment is a sign of heavy buildup, and a plastic valve may break if forced. Stop and call a plumber rather than force it. A tank that leaks from the valve after the flush may need a new valve, which is a quick job.

If the tank is old, rusty or noisy even after a flush, the better answer is water heater replacement. If you only need repair, see water heater repair.

Check the anode rod while you are there

The anode rod is a metal rod suspended inside the tank that corrodes in place of the steel. When it is worn away, the tank itself starts to corrode. Checking it every few years and replacing it when it is mostly gone can add years to the tank.

A rod that has lost most of its metal, or one that is coated with a hard crust, is ready for replacement. You need a socket wrench and some clearance above the tank, and a garage with a low ceiling can make the job awkward. If so, call us.

What a flush will not fix

A flush clears loose sediment. It does not fix a leaking tank, a failed element, a bad thermostat or a heavily corroded tank. If hot water is still lukewarm after a flush, another part is at fault and a technician should check it.

If the drain valve leaks after you close it, tighten it by hand or replace it. A plastic valve may need a brass one, which is a quick fix.

Gas and electric differences

On a gas tank, relight the pilot or reset the control after refilling, following the label. On an electric tank, make sure the tank is full before switching the breaker on, or the elements will burn out. In both cases, check the base and the drain for leaks during the first heat cycle.

Signs your tank needs a flush

Look for these signs at any time of year.

  • 1Popping, rumbling or banging sounds when the heater runs.
  • 2Rusty or cloudy hot water.
  • 3Less hot water than before.
  • 4A drain valve that is clogged or leaks.
  • 5An energy bill that has climbed with no change in use.

Keep a simple log

Write the date of each flush on the tank with a marker or in your phone. Note the condition of the water that came out and the state of the drain valve. Over time the log shows whether the tank is healthy or heading for failure.

Flushing in Duluth

Gwinnett County Water Resources supplies Duluth, and its annual water quality report is public. The report does not give a hardness number we can quote, so test your tap or ask the utility if you want to set a schedule based on the water. A yearly flush is a good default.

When you flush, send the water to a floor drain or the yard and keep it out of the street storm drains. Permit rules vary for replacing a tank, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office. We serve all of Duluth and nearby Peachtree Corners.

The U.S. Department of Energy guidelines recommend draining a quart of water from the tank every three months and flushing it yearly to control sediment.

How often should you flush?

Once a year is a practical routine for most homes, and every six months suits a house with a lot of sediment. If your heater makes popping noises, the water runs rusty or the hot water supply seems shorter, flush it sooner and check the anode rod as well.
