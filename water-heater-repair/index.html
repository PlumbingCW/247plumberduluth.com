No hot water or a leaking tank: what counts as urgent

A tank that leaks from the seam or the bottom is an emergency. Water is leaving the unit, and it can soak drywall, flooring and anything stored nearby. A unit that has simply stopped heating is less dangerous but still disruptive. Ask about timing when you call.

While you wait, switch off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or turn the gas control knob to pilot or off for a gas heater. Close the cold water valve on top of the tank, then open a hot faucet to relieve pressure. Never touch wiring if the floor is wet.

If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility before you call us. Our 24/7 line takes water heater calls at any hour once the house is safe.

Common failures and what they usually mean

Most water heater problems fall into a short list, and the symptom narrows the cause.

No hot water at all

On an electric unit this is often a tripped breaker, a failed upper heating element or a bad thermostat. On a gas unit it is often a pilot, thermocouple or gas valve problem.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast

A failed lower element, a broken dip tube or heavy sediment can all shorten your hot water supply.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment on the tank bottom traps water that boils and bursts through the layer. Flushing helps, but a heavily scaled tank may be near the end.

Water at the base of the tank

A loose fitting or a failed relief valve can be repaired. A leak from the tank body means replacement.

What a repair visit includes

The plumber confirms the model, age and fuel type, checks the power or gas supply, and tests the thermostat, elements or burner as they apply. We check the temperature and pressure relief valve, because a unit that leaks from that valve may be reporting excess pressure or heat.

Then you get the diagnosis and a clear price before any part is installed. If the repair costs too much against the age of the tank, we say so and show the replacement option on the water heater replacement page. If the unit is a tankless model, our tankless water heater service covers descaling and error codes.

Why water heaters in Duluth homes wear out

Duluth's housing boom ran through the 1980s and 1990s. Tanks installed with those homes have normally been replaced at least once, but second and third generation units installed around 2010 are now reaching their expected age of roughly 8 to 12 years.

Gwinnett County's tap water comes from Lake Lanier and is treated at the Shoal Creek and Lanier filter plants, and the county does not publish a hardness figure in its annual water quality report. Because hardness varies, we recommend testing your tap, and asking your water utility for its current report, before judging how fast scale will form in your tank.

Whatever your water is like, a yearly flush and an anode rod check extend tank life. We can do both during any repair visit, and we show you what we found on the tank.

Gas, electric and hybrid units

A gas heater needs combustion air and a proper vent, and a damaged flue can let carbon monoxide into the house. If a CO alarm sounds or the burner flame is yellow and lazy, leave the house and call 911 first. Electric units have no combustion risk but need correct breaker size. Hybrid heat pump units need clearance and condensate drainage, which we check on every repair.

Codes and standards behind the work

Water heater safety follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the manufacturer's installation instructions. Gas-fired tanks are also built to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard. When we repair a unit, we check the relief valve, the discharge pipe and the venting against those requirements.
