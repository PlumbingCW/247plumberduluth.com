When a failed tank needs replacing today

A water heater that leaks from the tank body, rusts at the seams or fills the drain pan cannot be repaired. Turn off the power or gas, close the cold water valve above the unit and call us. The line is open 24/7, and we talk through timing for after-hours emergencies.

Replacement is also the right call when the tank is past its expected life and has started to fail in several ways. A 10 to 12 year tank with a failed element, a rusty drain valve and a rumbling sound is usually near the end.

Gas smell means leave and call 911 or the gas utility first. Once the house is safe, we handle the rest.

How a replacement visit goes

You should know what happens before anyone enters your home.

  • 1Confirm the old tank's size, fuel type, venting and location, and check the gas line, shutoff valves and electrical supply.
  • 2Present the options that fit the space, such as another tank of the same capacity, a larger tank or a tankless unit, with a clear price for each.
  • 3Drain and disconnect the old unit, set the new one on a pan with a drain line when code and layout call for it, and connect new flexible lines and valves.
  • 4Fill, purge air, light or energize the unit, set the temperature, and test the relief valve and any expansion tank.
  • 5Haul the old tank away and walk you through the controls and the yearly maintenance.

Sizing, venting and code, in plain terms

Capacity depends on how many people shower back to back and whether you run a dishwasher and washer at the same time. A family of four often does well with a 50 gallon tank, while a couple may need less, and we size to your actual use.

Georgia plumbing work follows the state's plumbing code, which is built on the International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections staff, or Gwinnett County if you are in an unincorporated area, whether your swap needs one. We will tell you what we know and pull a permit when your jurisdiction requires it.

Relief valves, discharge piping, a drain pan where leaks could damage finished space, and a thermal expansion tank on a closed system are common code items. We explain which apply before the work starts so that the price you approve covers them.

Why so many Duluth tanks are due now

Duluth tripled in size in the 1980s and more than doubled again in the 1990s, according to Census counts, and many homes from that era have had a second or third tank since. Tanks installed 10 to 12 years ago across the city's subdivisions are now reaching the end of their normal life.

That helps you plan. A tank that is over 8 years old, sits in a garage or closet, and has never been flushed is worth replacing on your terms before it fails over a weekend. We schedule planned swaps, and we also handle emergencies. We serve every Duluth address, including ZIP codes 30095, 30096, 30097, 30098 and 30099.

If your tank is leaking right now

Turn off the power at the breaker or set the gas control to off, then close the cold water valve on top of the tank. If the leak is heavy, drain a few gallons through the hose valve to lower the water level, and move anything valuable away from the puddle.

Do not use the relief valve or touch a hot discharge pipe. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility before you call us. Take a photo of the leak and the rating label for your insurer, then call the 24/7 line.

Codes and standards behind the work

A replacement tank must meet the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers the relief valve, its discharge pipe, a drain pan where leaks could cause damage and expansion control on a closed system. We install to those requirements and the maker's instructions, and permit rules vary, so we confirm them for your address.
