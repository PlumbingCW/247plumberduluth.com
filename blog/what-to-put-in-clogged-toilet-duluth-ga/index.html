What to Put in a Clogged Toilet: A Duluth, GA Guide

Hot water, dish soap and a plunger solve most toilet clogs. Chemical drain cleaners and a pile of paper towels do not. Here is what works, what to skip and the signs that the problem is deeper.

Start with what is safe

A clogged toilet is rarely a disaster, but a wrong move can turn it into one. Stop flushing at once. If the bowl is close to the rim, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn off the shutoff valve behind the toilet, so water stops running.

Put on gloves, spread towels or a plastic sheet on the floor and make sure the bowl has some water in it. If the bowl is nearly empty, add water from a bucket to cover the drain opening, since a plunger works best underwater.

What to try, in order

Work from the gentlest fix to the strongest.

  • 1Hot water and dish soap. Squirt a good amount of dish soap into the bowl, wait a few minutes, then pour in a bucket of hot tap water from waist height. Do not use boiling water, which can crack porcelain.
  • 2Plunge properly. Use a flange plunger, which has a rubber extension that seals the drain. Push gently first to push out air, then pump firmly ten to twelve times.
  • 3Use a toilet auger. A closet auger reaches the trap. Feed it in, crank the handle to hook or break up the clog and pull it out.
  • 4Test the flush. Add water and flush once. If the water rises, stop and turn off the valve.
  • 5Repeat once. A stubborn clog may need two rounds before it moves.
  • 6Call if it fails. If the toilet will not clear, the clog is deeper than the trap.

What not to put in a clogged toilet

These common tricks cause more trouble than they solve.

Chemical drain cleaner

It can burn skin, damage the porcelain and wax seal, and leave a hazard for the next person who works on it.

Boiling water

It can crack a cold bowl and soften the wax seal.

Wire coat hangers

They scratch porcelain and snag in the trap.

Extra flushes

Each flush adds water that may spill over the rim.

Bleach and ammonia together

Mixing them makes dangerous gas.

A pile of toilet paper to absorb water

It can add to the clog.

Signs the clog is not just the toilet

If other drains gurgle, the tub backs up when you flush, or the water in the bowl rises when the washing machine drains, the clog is in the main sewer line. A plunger will not fix that, and repeated plunging may push sewage into the lowest drain in the house.

A toilet that clogs often may have a partial blockage, a vent problem or a worn flange. Our toilet repair service finds out why, and sewer line cleaning clears a blocked main.

How to use a plunger and an auger

A flange plunger works best because the extra rubber collar fits the drain opening. Seat it so the bowl is sealed, push down slowly the first time to avoid a splash, and then use short, firm strokes. After ten or so strokes, pull off the seal to see whether the water drops.

A closet auger, also called a toilet snake, has a curved head that will not scratch the bowl. Feed it into the drain with the guard against the porcelain, crank until you feel resistance and keep cranking to break up or snag the clog. Pull it out slowly and clean it.

Why some toilets clog more than others

Water-saving toilets use less water per flush, which is good for the bill, but a weak or worn model can leave waste in the trap. A toilet with a partly blocked rim or jet holes, or with a vent that is clogged, flushes poorly and clogs more.

Older homes with long horizontal runs under the floor see slow flow and more clogs. A camera check shows whether the line has a belly or roots. If the toilet clogs again right after you clear it, the cause is almost certainly further down.

Cleaning up safely after a clog

Wear gloves, wipe surfaces with a disinfectant and wash your hands well. Dry towels and mats and launder them hot. If sewage reached the floor beyond the bathroom, keep children and pets away until the area is cleaned and dried.

When a clogged toilet means call a plumber

A plunger and an auger solve most clogs, but these signs point to something bigger.

  • 1Water rises in a tub or shower when you flush.
  • 2Several drains gurgle at the same time.
  • 3The toilet clogs again within a day of clearing it.
  • 4An object is stuck and the auger does not retrieve it.
  • 5Sewage smell or water appears near the cleanout or floor drain.

Toilet clogs and the sewer in Duluth

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources operates the public sewer. The private lateral from the house to the public connection is the owner's responsibility, so a clog on your side is yours to clear. County guidelines also ask residents to put wipes, hygiene products and grease in the trash, and not down the drain.

Many Duluth homes date from the 1980s and 1990s, and roots can narrow the older laterals over time. If your toilet clogs more than a couple of times a year, ask for a camera check. We serve the whole city and nearby Peachtree Corners.

According to Gwinnett County guidelines, wipes marketed as flushable belong in the trash, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets the trap and vent rules that keep a toilet draining well.

How to prevent the next clog

Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Wipes sold as flushable, cotton balls, dental floss and paper towels do not break down quickly. Keep a plunger and a closet auger near the bathroom and teach everyone in the house how to use them.

If you have young children, a toilet lid lock helps stop toys from going in, and a clog caused by an object needs an auger or a pull rather than more water.
