What to do in the first five minutes

Shut off the main water valve. It is usually near where the line enters the house, in a garage, a utility room or a basement, or at the meter box in the front yard. Turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, call us and we will talk you through it.

Turn off power to any flooded area if you can do so safely from a dry spot, and stay out of standing water near outlets. Open faucets at the lowest point to drain the pipes, and move valuables up and out of the water.

Take photos of the damage for insurance before you begin cleanup. Then call our 24/7 line. For every burst pipe we talk through timing when you call, and we ask you to stay clear of the wet area until we arrive.

How we handle a burst pipe

The aim is to stop damage, then repair the cause, then leave the house working.

  • 1Confirm the shutoff. We make sure the water is fully off and drain the lines.
  • 2Find the break. We follow the wet area and test the pipe to locate the burst section.
  • 3Cut out the damage. The failed section is removed back to sound pipe.
  • 4Repair or replace. New pipe and fittings are installed and soldered, crimped or pressed.
  • 5Test and restore. We pressurize the line, check every joint and turn the water back on.
  • 6Advise on cleanup. We tell you what to dry out first and where a restoration company may help.

Why pipes burst in Georgia homes

Georgia is warm most of the year, but pipes still fail, and a hard freeze can catch a house unprepared.

Freezing

A cold snap can freeze pipes in attics, crawlspaces and exterior walls. Ice expands and splits the pipe, and the leak starts as it thaws.

High water pressure

Pressure above normal limits stresses joints and fixtures. A pressure reducing valve protects the system.

Corrosion and age

Old galvanized and some older copper pipes thin and fail.

Poor connections

Failed fittings and old repairs can blow apart under pressure.

Ground movement

Shifting soil can stress buried lines.

Water hammer

Sudden valve closure sends a shock wave through the pipes.

Frozen pipes in the Duluth area

Gwinnett County has cold snaps each winter, and Duluth homes built with pipes in exterior walls, attics or vented crawlspaces are the ones at risk. The National Weather Service advises keeping a trickle of water running, opening cabinet doors to warm exposed pipes and insulating anything in unheated space during a hard freeze.

If a pipe has frozen but not burst, leave the faucet open and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or heating pad. Never use an open flame. If you see a crack, a bulge or water coming through the wall, shut off the main and call us.

We repair burst lines in every Duluth neighborhood and in nearby Peachtree Corners. If the break shows that the supply line to the whole house is failing, see water line repair, and if several sections are worn out, whole-house repiping may be the better answer.

After the repair: water damage and insurance

Wet drywall, insulation and flooring should be dried within a day or two to prevent mold. A restoration company handles drying and removal, and we can tell you which areas are wet from what we saw during the repair.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden burst pipe damage. Photograph everything, keep your receipts and call your insurer early. We give you a written description of the repair for your claim.

Codes and standards behind the work

Repairs follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets approved pipe materials, joint methods and test requirements. New copper and PEX sections are made to published ASTM standards, and we pressure test every repair before the water is turned back on.
