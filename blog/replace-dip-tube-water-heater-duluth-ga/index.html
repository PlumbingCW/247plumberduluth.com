How to Replace a Water Heater Dip Tube in Duluth, GA

A dip tube is a simple plastic pipe inside the tank that has a big effect on hot water. When it fails, you get lukewarm showers and plastic flakes in the aerators. Here is how to spot it and fix it.

What a dip tube does

A dip tube is a plastic or metal pipe that runs from the cold water inlet at the top of the tank down toward the bottom. It sends incoming cold water to the bottom, where the burner or the lower element heats it. Hot water, which rises, leaves from the top.

Without the dip tube, cold water would enter at the top and mix with the hot water as it leaves. The tank would deliver lukewarm water, and you would run out sooner than expected, even though the heater is working.

Dip tubes are cheap and easy to forget, but they matter.

Signs the dip tube has broken

A failed dip tube is easy to mistake for a bad heating element or thermostat. These clues point to the tube.

  • 1Water is lukewarm even though the thermostat is set correctly and the burner or elements work.
  • 2The hot water runs out faster than it used to, with no change in use.
  • 3You find small white or gray plastic bits in faucet aerators, showerheads and appliance screens.
  • 4The tank is more than a few years old, or it dates from the mid-1990s when some manufacturers used a defective plastic.
  • 5The cold inlet nipple shows a broken or very short tube when you pull it out.

Replacing the dip tube

The job takes an hour or two. Gas and electric tanks are done almost the same way.

  • 1Power down. Turn off the electric breaker or set the gas valve to pilot, and close the cold inlet valve.
  • 2Drain a few gallons. Open a hot tap and drain the tank partway through the bottom valve so no water spills from the inlet.
  • 3Disconnect the inlet. Unscrew the cold water line and the heat trap nipple from the top of the tank.
  • 4Pull out the old tube. Remove the old tube and compare it to the length of the new one. A broken tube is shorter.
  • 5Install the new tube. Fit the new tube and tighten the fitting. Many nipples have the tube built in, so you replace the nipple.
  • 6Refill and flush. Reconnect the lines, fill the tank with a hot tap open, and flush the faucets to clear plastic debris.

Is it worth repairing an older tank?

A dip tube costs little, but the labor and the age of the tank decide the value. If the tank is under ten years old and otherwise sound, replacing the tube is cheap and effective. If it is older and shows rust or sediment, the money may be better spent on a new tank.

Why dip tubes failed in some older heaters

In the mid-1990s some manufacturers used a plastic dip tube that broke down in hot water. The tubes crumbled and left small flakes in the plumbing, and the heaters lost performance as cold water mixed with hot at the top of the tank. Lawsuits and replacements followed.

Modern dip tubes use better plastics, but the part is still plastic inside a hot tank, and any tank can have a worn one. If your heater is of that era, treat plastic bits in the faucets as a clue and look at the tube.

Cleaning up after a broken tube

Plastic flakes travel through the pipes and settle in screens. Remove each faucet aerator and each showerhead and rinse them, and check the filter screens on the washing machine hoses and the dishwasher inlet. Flush the lines by running each hot tap for a few minutes.

If the tank is full of fragments, flushing it will help. See our water heater repair page for what else we check during the visit.

Tools and parts checklist

Plan for a pipe wrench, a screwdriver, a garden hose, a new dip tube or heat trap nipple, thread seal tape or pipe dope and a bucket. Keep towels ready for drips. Check that the new tube matches the tank size, and that the fitting threads match the old one.

Signs it is time for a new tank instead

A new dip tube will not help a tank that has other problems.

  • 1Water leaks from the tank body or the bottom.
  • 2The tank is more than 10 to 12 years old and shows rust.
  • 3The water coming out is rusty or has a metallic smell.
  • 4The tank rumbles loudly even after flushing.
  • 5The pressure relief valve drips repeatedly.

How to check the tube without removing it

A quick test is to compare the temperature at the hot tap with the tank setting. If the tap runs lukewarm with the thermostat set at 120 degrees, and the elements or burner work, the tube is a likely cause. This test is not conclusive, so the tube should be seen before parts are bought.
