What a sewer inspection shows

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels through the line from a cleanout or a pulled toilet. It records as it goes, and a locator on the surface marks the depth and position of anything we find.

The footage shows the pipe material, the joints, roots coming through gaps, cracks, a section that has sagged or shifted, and any buildup of grease or scale. We pause on the problems and explain what each one means.

Nothing about the inspection is guesswork. If the line is sound, you know it. If it is not, you see the damage yourself and can judge the repair options.

When an inspection is worth booking

Most inspections happen for one of these reasons.

Buying a home

Sewer laterals are out of sight and expensive to fix. A camera check before closing gives you facts to negotiate with.

Repeat clogs

A line that backs up again and again has a cause. The camera finds it.

After a cleaning or jetting

A before and after video shows that the work succeeded.

Slow drains throughout the house

The camera checks whether the main line is narrowed or damaged.

Large trees near the line

Roots may be entering joints before any symptom appears.

Before a remodel or landscaping

Knowing the line's path and depth avoids damage during the project.

Inspections and the next step

If the camera finds only buildup, hydro jetting or sewer line cleaning clears it. If it finds a break, root intrusion at a joint or a collapsed section, sewer line repair is the answer, and the footage guides the plan.

We do not push repairs. A line with minor roots and no damage may need nothing beyond routine cleaning. A line with a long belly may need a full replacement. The camera makes the case either way.

Inspections for Duluth homes

Many Duluth homes date from the 1980s and 1990s. If you are buying one, a sewer scope is a sensible step because laterals of that age can hold roots, cracks and old materials. It also settles the question of whether the pipe needs work before you take ownership of it.

Gwinnett County's guidelines say the owner is responsible for the private lateral, up to and including the connection to the public sewer. That is the part we inspect. We scope lines across Duluth, including ZIP codes 30095 through 30099, and in nearby Peachtree Corners.

Reading the footage together

We pause the video on every defect and explain it in plain terms. A root intrusion at a joint is different from a crack along the pipe, and both differ from a belly that holds water. The location on the line matters too, because a problem near the house is cheaper to reach than one near the street.

You can ask us to replay any section, and you can send the file to a home inspector, a lender or your insurer. We note the pipe material on the report, since cast iron, clay and plastic each age in a different way and carry different repair options.

If the line is in good shape, we say so and tell you when a follow-up check would be reasonable. If it is not, we rank the findings so you can see what is urgent and what can wait.

Codes and standards behind the work

A camera inspection follows common trade practice, and the findings are measured against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) requirements for slope, joints and materials. We explain each finding in plain terms so you know whether it is a code issue, a wear issue or only buildup.
