When a leak needs a plumber now

Water you can see on a ceiling, a floor that feels warm or soft, or a sound of running water when everything is off all point to a leak that is active right now. Treat these as urgent. We answer 24/7 and talk through timing when you call, because wet drywall and subfloor start to fail within a day or two.

Shut off the main water valve while you wait, and turn off power to any wet area before you touch it. If a ceiling is bulging, poke a small hole in the lowest point over a bucket to let it drain, and keep people out from under it.

Do not guess the cause from the stain. Water travels along joists and pipes, and the wet spot is often several feet from the real break. Finding the true location is the entire purpose of a leak detection visit.

Symptoms that point to a hidden leak

Many calls begin with a symptom rather than a request for leak detection, and these are the ones we hear most.

The meter moves with every tap off

Water is leaving the house or the line somewhere. The leak could be inside, under the slab or in the yard.

A water bill much higher than normal

A running toilet is the commonest cause, but a leaking line is next. We test before guessing.

Ceiling or wall stains and peeling paint

A supply line, drain or shower pan may be seeping behind the finish.

A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water

A hot water line under a slab or under flooring can leak for months before anything shows.

Low water pressure with no clear cause

A leak before the fixtures can steal pressure. A pressure test narrows it down.

Soggy yard near the meter or the house

The line between the county meter and the house is the owner's side and a common failure point.

Check the meter yourself first

Gwinnett County Water Resources publishes a simple test you can do before we arrive, and it helps us work faster. The utility says nothing on the meter should move when no water is in use inside or outside the home.

  • 1Turn off the washing machine, dishwasher and ice maker, and ask everyone to avoid sinks, showers and toilets.
  • 2Find the meter under a metal cover in the front yard and note the reading or take a photo. Watch the small leak indicator wheel.
  • 3Wait two hours without using water, then read the meter again. A changed reading, or a spinning leak wheel, suggests a leak.
  • 4Add a teaspoon of food coloring to each toilet tank, do not flush, and check the bowl after 30 minutes. Color in the bowl means a worn flapper, the cheapest leak to fix.

How we find the leak without tearing out the house

A leak detection visit starts with isolating the system. We shut off fixtures and test which side of the house holds the problem. Then we use pressure testing, acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to follow the sound or the heat to a spot.

That spot is marked on the surface before anything is opened. A small, planned opening costs far less than exploratory demolition, and it keeps the repair focused. We also tell you if the evidence is inconclusive, because a guessed repair costs more than a second test.

For a leak that is entirely on your property, the repair can often be scheduled with the same visit. If the problem turns out to be a failing supply line, see water line repair, and for a burst section see burst pipe repair.

Slab leaks, crawlspace leaks and buried lines

A slab leak is a break in a pipe that runs under or inside a concrete foundation. Hot water lines are most often affected because heat and movement wear the pipe. Signs include a warm patch, the sound of running water and a bill that climbs. We confirm the line by isolating hot and cold, then pinpoint it before any concrete is cut.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks drip out of sight and can rot framing and feed mold. If your home has a crawlspace or a finished basement, we check the supply and drain lines overhead and look at insulation and vapor barrier for moisture.

Buried lines in the yard, including the line from the meter to the house and irrigation lines, are found with acoustic tools and by tracing the wet area. Irrigation leaks often show as a wet patch near a valve box, and a pool or spa leak is a different specialty that we can discuss on the phone.

Codes and standards behind the work

Pressure testing follows accepted plumbing practice, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets the test pressures used on new and repaired piping. According to Gwinnett County Water Resources, the meter test described above is the first step for any suspected leak, and we start there too.
