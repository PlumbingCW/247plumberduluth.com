Tankless Water Heater Life: A Duluth, GA Homeowner Guide

Tankless water heaters often outlast tanks, but only when they are installed and serviced well. Learn what affects their life, the yearly care that matters and when a replacement is the better call.

How long a tankless water heater lasts

The U.S. Department of Energy says tankless water heaters can last more than 20 years, and often outlast storage tanks by a good margin. Many makers describe a service life in the range of 15 to 20 years with proper care. These are best-case figures, and neglect shortens them.

A tankless unit has parts that can be replaced, including the flow sensor, the igniter and the fan. That is a reason it can last so long, since a repair can restore it. A storage tank, in contrast, usually fails when the tank itself corrodes through.

Your own result depends on the water, the installation and the care you give it.

What shortens the life of a tankless unit

Most early failures trace back to a short list of causes.

Scale

Minerals narrow the heat exchanger and cause overheating. Flushing prevents this.

No yearly service

A neglected unit builds up scale and debris, and a warranty may require records.

Poor venting

Wrong vent length or material causes errors and corrosion.

Undersized gas line

Low gas pressure causes weak flames and shutdowns.

Freezing

A unit in an unheated space can freeze and crack in a hard cold snap.

Wrong size

A unit that is too small runs at maximum much of the time and wears faster.

Yearly care that adds years

These tasks fit into one service visit.

  • 1Flush the heat exchanger with the maker's recommended solution, usually once a year.
  • 2Clean the inlet water filter screen.
  • 3Inspect the vent and the air intake for blockage or damage.
  • 4Check the gas pressure and the connections.
  • 5Read the error history and check the condensate drain on condensing models.
  • 6Keep service records for any warranty claim.

Repair or replace an aging tankless unit

A unit that is under ten years old and needs a part such as a sensor or an igniter is usually worth repairing. A unit with a damaged heat exchanger past the warranty is a closer call, and a tankless unit that has had years of scale buildup may keep failing.

We give a written comparison of repair and replacement so you can decide. See tankless water heater for service and water heater installation if you are planning a new unit.

Sizing and use affect life

A tankless heater that is correctly sized runs at a moderate level most of the time. One that is too small is pushed to its limit whenever two showers run, which strains parts and cuts life. Sizing depends on the flow rate you need and the temperature rise from the incoming water to the set point.

Winter groundwater is colder, and the same heater must work harder in January than in July. Ask for sizing based on the winter condition and your peak use, not just the number of bathrooms.

Warranty and records

Many warranties on heat exchangers run for a decade or more, but they often require installation by a licensed plumber and proof of maintenance. Keep receipts, the model and serial numbers and the dates of each flush. Georgia plumbers can be verified through the Secretary of State's plumbers division.

If a claim becomes necessary, a clean paper trail makes it easier. We write each visit up so you have a record.

Keep the manual and the model number

Store the owner's manual, the model number and the serial number where you can find them. Photograph the rating plate on the unit. When a technician calls about parts or the warranty, those details save time, and they help you compare the age of your unit against the maker's stated service life.

Questions to ask before you replace

Ask the technician what failed and whether a repair would last. Ask for the age of the unit and the cost of parts. Ask how the new unit would be sized and vented, and whether the gas line must change. These answers let you compare a repair and a replacement on equal terms, and they keep a surprise from appearing on the invoice.

Signs a tankless heater is near the end of its life

These signs, taken together, point toward replacement.

  • 1Repeated error codes after service.
  • 2Temperature swings that do not improve after a flush.
  • 3Visible rust, water or soot inside the cabinet.
  • 4Parts that are costly or no longer available.
  • 5A heat exchanger leak.

Planning a replacement

Do not wait for a failure in January. If the unit is old and showing problems, plan a replacement in a calm month. We can size the new unit, check venting and gas supply and schedule the work around your day.
