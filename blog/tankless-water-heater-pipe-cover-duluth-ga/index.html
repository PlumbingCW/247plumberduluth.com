Tankless Water Heater Pipe Cover: A Duluth, GA Homeowner Guide

Insulating the hot water pipes and the unit's connections saves heat and protects against cold snaps. The covering has to be the right type and kept away from hot parts. Here is how to choose and install it.

Why pipe covers matter on a tankless system

A tankless heater makes hot water on demand, but the pipe between the unit and the tap still holds water that cools between uses. Insulating the hot pipe slows that heat loss and shortens the wait for hot water at the tap. The U.S. Department of Energy recommends insulating hot water pipes to reduce heat loss and save energy.

Cover is also a form of protection. A cold snap can freeze a pipe in an unheated garage or an exterior wall, and a layer of insulation buys time. It is not a guarantee, so keep the freeze protection in the unit's manual in place.

Common pipe cover materials

The right material depends on the location and the temperature.

MaterialWhere it fitsWatch for
Foam tube sleevesIndoor hot and cold lines in garages, crawlspaces and atticsNot for high heat near flues
Fiberglass wrapHot pipes where space allowsNeeds a jacket and gloves to install
Heat tape with insulationExposed pipes in unheated areasNeeds the right model and a safe power connection
Rubber insulationPipes that need moisture resistanceCosts more than foam

Where and how to install a pipe cover

These are the steps most homes follow.

  • 1Start at the heater. Cover the first few feet of hot water pipe leaving the unit, but leave the connection fittings and valves accessible.
  • 2Keep clear of vents. Never wrap or cover the exhaust vent, the air intake or any part the manual says must stay open.
  • 3Cut the sleeves to length. Slit foam sleeves lengthwise and slide them on, then seal the seams with tape.
  • 4Insulate elbows and valves. Use mitered pieces on corners and leave handles free.
  • 5Cover pipes in cold spaces. Garages, attics, crawlspaces and exterior walls come first.
  • 6Check the work. Look for gaps, then feel the pipe after a use. It should stay warm longer.

What to avoid

Never put insulation on the vent pipe of a gas tankless unit unless the maker says so. The exhaust can be hot and a cover could trap heat or block air. Do not cover the condensate line on a condensing model if the maker warns against it, and do not wrap the relief valve discharge.

Do not use heat tape that is not rated for the pipe material, and never wrap heat tape over itself. If a pipe has frozen and burst, see burst pipe repair. For installation of the unit itself, see tankless water heater.

Recirculation and wait time

Pipe insulation shortens the time hot water stays warm in the line, but it does not remove the wait for the first hot water. If waiting is the real complaint, a recirculation pump and a return line can keep hot water near the tap. Some tankless models have a built-in recirculation function.

Recirculation adds cost and uses some energy, so size it for a long run or a far bathroom rather than the whole house. We can talk through it when we review your system.

Check the freeze protection on your unit

Many tankless heaters have built-in freeze protection that uses a small heater inside the unit. It works only if the unit has power, so a power outage during a freeze can leave it exposed. Note the minimum temperature in the manual and keep the unit plugged in.

For gas models in unheated spaces, ask how the condensate line is protected. A frozen condensate line can shut the unit down.

Checking the work after a winter cold snap

After a hard freeze, look at the covered pipes for gaps, torn foam and wet spots. Feel along the hot line for warmth, and listen for dripping near joints. Replace any covering that has come loose or soaked up water, since wet foam stops insulating. A quick check each fall and spring keeps the covering doing its job.

Pipe cover mistakes to avoid

A few errors reduce the benefit or create hazards.

  • 1Covering vents, intakes or the pressure relief discharge.
  • 2Leaving gaps at elbows and valves where heat escapes.
  • 3Using foam near hot flue pipes where it can melt.
  • 4Wrapping heat tape over itself or over insulation not rated for it.
  • 5Skipping the cold lines in an unheated garage.

Paying attention to the first few feet

The first few feet out of the heater lose the most heat, so insulate them first. Use a material rated for the temperature near the unit and keep the covering back from the cabinet and the vent by the distance the manual requires.

Insulating pipes in Duluth

Georgia winters are mild most years, but hard freezes do occur. The National Weather Service advises protecting exposed pipes during cold snaps, which in practice means insulating pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls and letting a faucet drip on the coldest nights.

Many Duluth tankless units are in garages. A garage that is not heated will drop close to outside temperatures, so pipe covers matter there. We install and service tankless systems in Duluth and in nearby Peachtree Corners.

The U.S. Department of Energy guidelines recommend insulating hot water pipes, and the manufacturer's manual sets the clearances around vents.

Cost and payback

Foam sleeves cost little and take an afternoon to install. The savings are modest, but real, and they are larger on long runs between the heater and a distant bathroom. If you wait a long time for hot water, a recirculation line may help more than insulation alone.
