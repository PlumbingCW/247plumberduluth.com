How to Detect Plumbing Leaks in Duluth, GA

Most leaks give signals before they cause a flood. This guide walks through the checks a homeowner can do in an afternoon, from the water meter to the toilet tank, and when to call for help.

Why leaks go unnoticed

Most plumbing leaks are out of sight. They drip inside walls, under floors and in the yard, and the first sign is often a higher water bill or a stain. By the time you see damage, water has been escaping for weeks.

A systematic check works better than guessing. Start with the meter, which tells you if any water is leaving the system at all, then narrow the search to the toilets, the fixtures, the water heater and the yard.

The meter test

Gwinnett County Water Resources describes a simple test. The meter should not move when no water is in use.

  • 1Stop all water use. Turn off the dishwasher, washing machine, ice maker and irrigation, and make sure no one is using a tap.
  • 2Find the meter. It sits under a metal or plastic lid in the front yard or at the curb. Lift the lid carefully, watching for insects.
  • 3Record the reading. Note the digits or take a photo, and look at the small leak indicator on the dial.
  • 4Wait two hours. Do not use water during this time.
  • 5Read it again. If the numbers have moved or the leak indicator is spinning, water is leaking somewhere.

Room-by-room checks

If the meter moved, these are the next places to look.

Toilets

Add food coloring to the tank and wait 30 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leaking flapper.

Sinks and tubs

Look under cabinets for dampness, rust on pipes and swollen wood.

Water heater

Check the base for water and the relief valve for drips or mineral crust.

Appliances

Check hoses behind the washer, the dishwasher and the refrigerator ice maker.

Walls and ceilings

Look for stains, bubbling paint and a musty smell below bathrooms and above kitchens.

Yard and meter box

Look for soggy ground, unusually green grass or water in the meter box with all water off.

Slab and wall leaks need professional tools

If the meter moves, the toilets are fine and nothing is visibly wet, the leak may be inside a wall, under a slab or in a buried line. A warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a bill that climbs all point to a slab leak, which usually involves a hot water line.

Our leak detection service uses pressure tests, acoustic equipment and thermal imaging to find the exact spot before anything is cut open. If the leak is on the line from the meter to the house, see water line repair.

Reading your water bill for clues

Your bill is a free leak detector. Compare the last several months, and note any climb that does not match a change in your household or the season. A steady rise over several bills suggests a slow leak, and a sudden jump suggests something new.

Winter usage in a house without an irrigation system should be fairly flat. If your winter use is much higher than your neighbors' with a similar household, a hidden leak is worth ruling out with a meter test.

Leaks that hide in plain sight

Toilets are the biggest hidden water wasters, because a leaking flapper can run for weeks without sound. Irrigation systems come next, since a broken head or a stuck valve can run when no one is watching. Check zones one at a time and look for soggy patches.

Pools, spas and outdoor faucets are also common. A hose bib with a drip or a hose left on a timer can add hundreds of gallons. Walk the yard after a dry spell and look for green strips or sunken ground that mark a buried line.

Keep a simple leak log

Write down the date, the meter reading and what you tested. A log makes the next call faster, since we can see when the problem started and what you ruled out. It also gives your insurer a clear record if a claim becomes necessary.

Take photos of the meter dial, any stains and the water heater base. If the leak turns out to be inside a wall, the photos help us decide where to start the search.

When to call a plumber right away

Some signs mean the leak is serious or hidden, and a visit should not wait.

  • 1The meter keeps moving after you have checked every toilet and fixture.
  • 2A ceiling is sagging or a wall is wet and soft.
  • 3You hear running water with everything off.
  • 4A section of the floor is warm or damp, with no clear source.
  • 5The water bill has doubled and you cannot explain it.

Why small leaks deserve attention

A slow leak can rot framing, ruin flooring and feed mold for months before you notice. The water bill is the least of the cost. Insurers often treat long-term seepage differently from sudden damage, so catching it early protects your claim.

A leak that you can fix with a part costing a few dollars becomes a major repair when ignored. Check the usual suspects each season.

Water meters and leaks in Duluth

In Duluth, Gwinnett County Water Resources owns the meter and the pipe that connects it to the water main. The owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house, and for everything inside. If you see water flowing at the meter itself on the street side, the county should be notified.

The county also publishes tips on finding leaks and reading your meter, and you can check your bill history for sudden jumps. Keep your dated meter photos, because they help show the utility and an insurer when the leak began. We serve all of Duluth and nearby Peachtree Corners.

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, household leaks waste a large amount of water every year, and a toilet is the most common source.

What to do once you find a leak

If water is actively escaping, shut off the main valve and call a plumber. Dry wet materials within a day or two to prevent mold, and take photos for your insurer. Many homeowner policies cover sudden leaks and may not cover the slow ones, so read your policy.

Even a small drip should be fixed. A dripping fixture wastes water and often points to a part that will fail completely later.
