Signs the line itself has failed

A clog clears and stays clear. A damaged line keeps coming back. If several fixtures gurgle at once, a floor drain backs up after laundry, or the yard has a wet and foul patch over the line, the pipe may be cracked, offset or collapsed.

This is an emergency whenever sewage reaches the house. Our 24/7 line takes these calls, and the first goal is to open the line and stop the backup. The repair is then planned from what the camera shows.

Until we arrive, stop running water, keep children and pets out of the affected room and do not pour chemical cleaner down the drain. It will not fix a broken pipe and it puts the next plumber at risk.

What typically damages a sewer line

Most sewer lateral failures trace back to a short list of causes, and knowing which one applies decides the repair.

Tree roots

Roots enter at joints and widen them. Georgia red clay and mature trees make this common on older lots.

Cast iron corrosion

Cast iron drains rust from the inside and flake. The pipe narrows and eventually breaks.

Clay and fiber pipe

Older clay joints shift and fiber conduit softens. Both can collapse.

Soil movement

Heavy rain, washouts and settling can pull a line out of alignment and create a belly that holds water.

Heavy loads

A driveway or equipment over a shallow line can crush it.

Poor earlier repairs

Mismatched fittings or patched sections can leak or catch debris.

How a sewer line repair is planned

We keep the work in a clear order so you know what is happening and why.

  • 1Stop the backup. We open the line enough to relieve sewage and make the property safe.
  • 2Camera inspection. A video of the full line shows the break, the roots and the pipe material.
  • 3Locate and measure. We mark the depth and the run on the surface with a locator.
  • 4Choose the method. Trenchless, spot repair or full replacement is explained with prices.
  • 5Repair and test. The new section is joined, tested and backfilled.
  • 6Final camera. We run the camera again to confirm the line is sound.

Sewer rules and ownership in Duluth

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources runs the public sewer system. The county's guidelines say the owner is responsible for the private lateral that connects the building to the public sewer, up to and including the connection. If the failure is on your side, the repair is yours to arrange.

Permit and inspection requirements vary by project, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office what applies to your address. Digging also requires a utility locate call to Georgia 811 before any work begins, and we handle that call.

A sewer backwater valve can protect a home on a low lot from a public main surcharge. We can discuss whether one fits your house when we review the camera footage.

Why older Duluth lines need attention

A large share of Duluth homes were built during the growth years of the 1980s and 1990s, which means their laterals are now 25 to 45 years old. Pipe materials of that era vary, and some neighborhoods have cast iron or clay sections near the house.

Mature oaks and pines along many streets add root pressure. We repair lines at homes and businesses across Duluth, in all of ZIP codes 30095 through 30099, and in nearby Peachtree Corners.

Codes and standards behind the work

Building sewer work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets minimum slope, pipe materials and joint requirements. Pipe materials used in repairs are made to published ASTM standards, and we choose the material to suit the soil and the depth.
