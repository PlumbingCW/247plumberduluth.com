Signs your water line is failing

A wet, sunken or unusually green patch in the yard, especially between the meter and the house, is the classic sign. Others are low pressure throughout the house, a meter that spins with everything off, a sudden jump in the water bill, and the sound of running water outside.

A complete break means no water at all, and a major leak may flood the yard. Either is an emergency, and we answer 24/7. Shut off the water at the main valve in the house, or at the meter if you can, and call us.

If you can see flowing water at the meter itself, that belongs to the county. Gwinnett County Water Resources owns the meter and the pipe from the water main to the meter, and the owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house.

Why water lines fail

Water lines are buried and out of sight, so failures usually appear only after months of slow damage.

Age and corrosion

Older galvanized and some copper lines corrode from the inside and the outside.

Soil conditions

Georgia clay holds water and shifts, which stresses pipes.

Tree roots

Roots can crush or wrap a line.

High pressure

Pressure above the normal range wears joints.

Poor installation

Shallow burial, bad bends and weak fittings fail early.

Past damage

Construction and digging can nick a line and cause a later leak.

Water line work in Duluth

Duluth homes built in the 1980s and 1990s often have a service line that is now decades old. We test for leaks, check pressure and review the pipe material before we recommend repair or replacement. Pressure above about 80 psi is hard on a house, and a pressure reducing valve at the main protects fixtures and the water heater.

We call Georgia 811 before any digging and coordinate with the county when the work touches the meter. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office what applies to your address. We repair lines across Duluth and in Peachtree Corners, and serve all ZIP codes from 30095 to 30099.

What to expect on repair day

On the day of the work we confirm the shutoff, locate the line with a tracer or a locator, and mark the dig area. We protect the lawn and the driveway where we can, and we put excavated soil on a tarp so it can go back where it came from.

Water service is off while the line is open. A spot repair can take a few hours, and a longer replacement may take most of a day. We tell you the expected shutoff time ahead so you can fill containers and plan around it.

When the new pipe is in, we flush the line, test for leaks and check the pressure at a hose bib. Then we backfill, compact the soil and clean up. If the dig crossed a lawn or a hardscape, we explain what restoration is included.

Codes and standards behind the work

Water service lines are governed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the local water authority's rules for materials, depth and connections. Pipe used for service lines is made to published ASTM and AWWA standards, and we confirm the requirements with the county when the work touches the meter.
