Why commercial plumbing needs a different approach

Commercial buildings have larger pipes, more fixtures, backflow devices, grease handling and rules that residential plumbing does not. A fault that takes a day to fix at a house can shut a restaurant, so timing matters.

We take emergency calls 24/7 and talk through timing when you call. When you call, tell us whether the issue is a sewage backup, a burst pipe, a failed water heater or a flooded restroom, and whether the building is open. We start with the fastest safe way to stop the damage.

For planned work, we schedule outside business hours when we can. Our price comes before the work, and we explain the plan so you can brief your staff.

Grease, backflow and local rules for Duluth businesses

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources runs a Fats, Oils and Grease program for food service establishments. It sets rules for grease interceptors and traps, and it sets maintenance and recordkeeping requirements. Ask the county about the rules for your type of operation.

Georgia also requires backflow prevention devices on many commercial connections to protect the public water supply. Devices are tested on a schedule, and the water provider sets the testing rules. We test, repair and replace devices, and we help you file results with the right office.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office about your project. We pull permits where they apply.

Working with property managers and owners

Property managers need records. After each visit we describe what we found, what we did and what should be watched, so that you have a clear entry for your files. If a tenant is responsible for a problem, the report helps you sort that out.

For multi-unit buildings we separate unit-level problems from shared-line problems. A clog in one unit is a local repair, while a backup that reaches several units points to the main line, and the repair plan changes. We check the building's cleanouts and tell you where they are.

We can agree on a routine visit schedule for properties that want preventive checks of water heaters, backflow devices and main lines. Call and describe the building and we will outline what makes sense.

Codes and standards behind the work

Commercial work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, and backflow devices must meet standards published by ASSE International. Gwinnett County also publishes guidelines for grease control in food service. We build each repair and each test around those documents.
