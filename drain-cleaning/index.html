When a clogged drain is an emergency

A slow sink can wait for a morning appointment. A toilet that overflows, a tub that fills with water from the washing machine, or a floor drain that bubbles up is a different situation, and it needs a plumber today. We take emergency drain calls 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Ask about timing when you call.

While you wait, stop using every fixture in the house. Water you add keeps flowing toward the blockage and then toward your floor. If a toilet is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the small valve behind the toilet.

Call straight away if more than one fixture is affected. A sink that gurgles when the shower drains, or a toilet that bubbles when the dishwasher runs, usually points to the main line rather than a single branch.

What to do before the plumber arrives

We can talk you through the first steps, and most of them take under a minute. They also keep a small problem from becoming a flooded room.

Stop adding water

No flushing, no showers, no laundry and no dishwasher cycles until the line is clear.

Skip the chemical cleaner

Caustic drain products sit in a standing clog and can splash on anyone working on the pipe. Plain plunging is safer.

Protect the floor

Put towels at the base of a rising toilet and lift rugs and boxes away from a floor drain.

Find your cleanout

A capped pipe in the yard or garage wall is the fastest access point for a main line. Tell us if you can see one.

Reading the symptoms

Where the water backs up tells us where to start. Describing the pattern on the phone saves time once the truck arrives.

One sink drains slowly

This is usually a local clog in the trap or the branch pipe, often hair, soap or grease. Cabling the branch normally clears it.

Tub and toilet back up together

Fixtures sharing a branch point to a clog downstream of both. We run a cable or camera into that branch.

Every drain gurgles

Air and water are fighting a blockage in the main line, which is more serious than a single fixture clog.

The same drain clogs every few weeks

A repeat clog means something is left behind, such as grease on pipe walls, roots, or a belly in the line. A camera shows which.

How we clear a drain

We start with the least invasive tool that fits the problem. A hand auger or a drum machine with a cutting head clears most sink, tub and branch clogs. A cable with a larger head can cut through roots in a main line and pull back debris.

When cable work only opens a hole through the blockage, the line clogs again soon after. In that case we recommend hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water. If the cause is unclear, a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe from the inside, so you see the root, the offset joint or the grease buildup before you pay for a fix.

We also tell you plainly when drain cleaning is the wrong answer. A collapsed or heavily cracked line will not stay clear, and the honest next step is a repair, which we cover on the sewer line repair page.

Why drains clog in Duluth homes

Census counts show Duluth growing from roughly 3,000 residents in 1980 to more than 22,000 by 2000. A large share of the city's houses and condos are therefore between 25 and 45 years old, which is long enough for grease films, mineral scale and tree roots to narrow the original drain lines.

Kitchens cause most repeat clogs. Gwinnett County Water Resources tells residents not to pour grease or food scraps down the drain even when a garbage disposal is installed, and to put fats and cooking oil in a lidded container in the trash. The county also notes that a clog on your own property is the owner's responsibility, so prevention saves you a visit.

Duluth's older neighborhoods near downtown and the newer subdivisions along Pleasant Hill Road, Peachtree Industrial Boulevard and the Buford Highway corridor all send us calls, and we serve Duluth addresses under ZIP codes 30095, 30096, 30097, 30098 and 30099. The mix of ages means we check the pipe material and the cleanout before choosing a tool.

Codes and standards behind the work

Drain work is shaped by the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets rules for traps, vents and drain sizing. Georgia adopts the IPC with state amendments for its plumbing code. We follow those principles when we open a line, and we tell you if a venting or trap problem is causing the clog.

Drains in businesses and multifamily buildings

Restaurants, salons and apartment buildings put heavier loads on drain lines, and grease interceptors have their own county rules. If a business line is slow, see our commercial plumbing page. For a landlord with a tenant on the line, we can talk to the property manager directly and schedule around access.
