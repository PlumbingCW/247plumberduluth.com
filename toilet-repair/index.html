If the toilet is overflowing right now

Take the lid off the tank and push the flapper down into its seat, or lift the float, to stop more water entering the bowl. Then turn the shutoff valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. That stops the flow in seconds.

Do not flush again. Put towels around the base and keep children away from the water, which may be contaminated if the drain is backing up. If water also rises in a tub or a shower, the clog is deeper than the toilet, and you should call us right away.

Our 24/7 line takes toilet emergencies, and we talk through timing when you call. We clear the clog, repair the leak or replace the toilet, and leave the bathroom dry and safe to use.

Toilet problems we fix

Most toilet calls fall into a handful of categories, and each has a different repair.

Overflow and clogs

We clear the trap with a closet auger and find out why it clogged.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt lets water seep under the toilet and rot the floor.

Constantly running

A worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a wrong float level wastes water.

Weak flush

Mineral buildup in the rim jets or a partial clog reduces flush strength.

Rocking toilet

A toilet that moves has a failing seal and often damage underneath.

Cracked tank or bowl

Cracks leak and cannot be repaired, so the toilet must be replaced.

How we repair a leaking toilet base

A leak at the base is one of the most common calls, and the fix is the same each time.

  • 1Shut off and drain. We close the supply and empty the tank and bowl.
  • 2Lift the toilet. The toilet is disconnected and set aside on a protected surface.
  • 3Inspect the floor. We check the flange and the subfloor for rot or damage.
  • 4Install a new seal. A new wax ring or a modern seal goes on, with new bolts if needed.
  • 5Reset and test. The toilet is set level, tightened and flushed several times.
  • 6Check for leaks. We watch the base for dampness after the test.

Repair or replace the toilet

A repair makes sense for a worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a bad seal. Replacement makes sense when the porcelain is cracked, when repeated repairs add up or when an older high-volume toilet wastes water.

Federal rules limit new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush, and many models use 1.28 gallons or less. A newer toilet can lower water use noticeably. Gwinnett County also runs a water conservation program that encourages efficient fixtures. We bring common replacement toilets on the truck, and we haul away the old one.

If the clog keeps coming back, the problem may be in the line behind the toilet, and drain cleaning or a sewer line cleaning visit may be needed.

Keeping a toilet trouble-free

Flush only toilet paper and waste. Wipes sold as flushable, cotton products and paper towels do not break down quickly and are the leading cause of toilet and main-line clogs we see.

Check the tank once a year. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait half an hour and look for color in the bowl. If it appears, the flapper leaks and a small repair will stop a wasteful and expensive trickle.

Replace the flexible supply line and the shutoff valve when they show corrosion or when the toilet is replaced. A burst supply line is a common source of flooding and is cheap to prevent.

Codes and standards behind the work

Toilets sold in the United States are limited to 1.6 gallons per flush by federal rule, and the EPA's WaterSense program labels models at 1.28 gallons or less. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) also sets standards for flange height and seal. We follow these when we reset or replace a toilet.
