Where the Hill Community sits in Duluth

The Hill Community is a small residential neighborhood near the center of Duluth. The City of Duluth's 2012 neighborhood plan defines it by six anchor streets: Church Street, Washington Street, Donaville Street, Dogwood Street, Jones Street and Apartment Drive. Some properties that front Buford Highway are included as well.

The plan calls it an established, mostly residential area, with a few structures that date to 1878. News coverage in 2023 also described the Hill as a historic community with long family ties. This page sticks to plumbing information and does not draw any boundary beyond what the city plan states.

Maps can disagree about where a neighborhood ends. Give us your street when you call and we will confirm that the address is in our service area.

What the city plan recorded about the homes

When the city wrote its plan, it counted 54 housing units in the neighborhood, and about 46 percent were owner occupied. The average structure was 40.5 years old, a figure the plan says is pulled upward by the two historic structures. Those are 2012 numbers, so read them as a snapshot and not as the condition of any single house.

The age of a house does not tell you what is inside its walls. Only an inspection shows whether a line is copper, galvanized steel, plastic or something else. We do not guess about your pipes from the year a house was built, and we explain what we find before you decide on a repair.

The sewer project and why it matters for plumbing

The 2012 plan listed the lack of sewer service as a weakness of the neighborhood. In September 2021 the city announced that it would use American Rescue Plan Act funds to bring sanitary sewer to the Hill and nearby Pinecrest, with Gwinnett County paying part of the construction cost. In September 2023 the city council authorized eminent domain for easements, and the city said the work would replace aging septic systems.

Gwinnett County Water Resources then bid a construction contract titled Duluth Hill Area Sewer System in 2024. Public listings describe about 4,600 linear feet of 8 inch gravity sewer, a pump station and water main work. We could not confirm from public sources which individual properties are connected today, so ask the county about your address.

Questions that come up when a street moves from septic to sewer

These are general plumbing points, not a claim about any particular house.

Know which system you have

A septic tank has a buried lid in the yard, while a sewer home is billed for sewer service. If you are unsure, ask Gwinnett County Water Resources.

Ask about the old tank

The county or the city sets the rules for what happens to a tank once a home connects. Ask before anyone digs or fills anything.

Find your cleanout

The cleanout is the capped pipe that gives a plumber access to the line from the house. Knowing where it is saves time on any visit.

Watch for warning signs

Slow drains, gurgling fixtures and wet or smelly spots in the yard can point to a problem in the line. Stop heavy water use and call.

Get the line checked

A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe from the house toward the connection point before you spend money on a repair.

Water, sewer and who owns what

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources supplies water and runs the public sewer in Duluth. In general, the owner is responsible for the private line that leaves the house, and the county handles the meter and the public main. A plumber can tell you which side of that line a problem sits on, which is worth knowing before you pay for digging.

If water seems to be running when nothing is on, leak detection finds the source without opening walls first. If the break is on the line from the meter, water line repair covers that work. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth what applies to your address.

Everyday plumbing help for homes in the Hill

Most calls from any neighborhood fall into a few groups. Drain cleaning handles slow or backed up lines, water heater repair covers a tank that has stopped heating, and toilet repair fixes fixtures that run or leak.

Those are general services and not a statement about what fails most often in the Hill. Tell us what you see and hear, and we talk through the likely cause and the next step.
