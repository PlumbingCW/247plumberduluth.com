Installing a Kitchen Garbage Disposal in Duluth, GA

Installing a disposal looks easy, and often is, if the sink and wiring cooperate. This guide covers what to buy, the steps in order and the points where homeowners usually call for help.

Before you buy a disposal

Start by checking the sink. Most kitchen sinks have a standard 3.5 inch drain opening that fits a disposal flange, but some sinks and drains do not. A double-bowl sink needs the disposal on the bowl with the drain, and the other bowl connects through a tailpiece.

Next, check the power. A disposal needs a nearby outlet or a hardwired connection with a switch. If there is no power under the sink, you will need an electrician or a plumber who does this work. Also check space, because a big disposal may not fit under a shallow sink.

Horsepower is the last choice. A half-horsepower unit handles light use, and a three-quarter or one horsepower model grinds tougher scraps and runs more quietly.

These are the main trade-offs.

Installation steps in order

Always turn off the power at the breaker, not just the switch.

  • 1Remove the old unit or drain. Disconnect the old disposal or the sink strainer and clean the sink opening.
  • 2Set the mounting ring. Apply plumber's putty under the new flange, fit the mounting ring and tighten it from below.
  • 3Connect the wiring. Run the cord or the wire through the access plate and connect it to the power with wire nuts.
  • 4Hang the disposal. Lift the unit onto the mounting ring and twist it until it locks.
  • 5Connect the drain. Attach the discharge tube to the trap and the dishwasher hose to the inlet, after knocking out the plug if a dishwasher is connected.
  • 6Test for leaks. Restore power, run water and check each joint with the unit on and off.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if the sink drain is a different size, the trap needs changes, the wiring is missing or the cabinet leaks already. A hand-tightened joint that weeps is a common call-back, and a plumber knows the correct seal for each fitting.

Our garbage disposal repair page covers fixing a unit you already have, and plumbing installation covers new fixtures and appliances.

Planning the electrical side

A disposal runs on household power, usually 120 volts on its own circuit or a shared kitchen circuit. Many are wired directly to a switch and a junction box, and some use a plug and a switched outlet under the sink. Either way, the connection must be safe and accessible.

If you need a new outlet or a switch, hire an electrician or a plumber who handles that work. Permit rules vary for new wiring, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office before you start.

Dishwasher connections and common leaks

If a dishwasher drains into the disposal, its hose connects at a small inlet on the side. That inlet has a knockout plug that must be removed first, or the dishwasher will not drain. Forgetting this step is a classic reason a new install fails its first test.

Most leaks after installation come from three places: the sink flange where the putty did not seal, the discharge tube gasket and the trap connections. Run the water and check each with a dry paper towel before closing the cabinet.

What to expect from the finished job

A good install is quiet and leak-free, and the sink drains freely. Run cold water through the unit for a full minute at the end and watch the trap. If it is slow, the problem is further down the line and cleaning will help.

Keep the old unit's manual and the new warranty with your records, and note the date. If you ever need service, a plumber will want the model number.

Common installation mistakes

Avoid these and the job goes smoothly.

  • 1Skipping the plumber's putty under the sink flange, which causes a slow leak.
  • 2Forgetting to knock out the dishwasher plug.
  • 3Over-tightening the mounting ring and cracking the sink.
  • 4Using wire nuts that are too small, which loosen over time.
  • 5Not testing with the cabinet open before packing the space under the sink again.

Disposals and drains in Duluth

Gwinnett County asks households to keep fats, oils and grease out of drains, and a disposal does not change that rule. Grease goes through the disposal and then coats the drain line and the sewer lateral, which causes many of the clogs we clear.

Duluth homes with older drain lines should be cautious. If your kitchen drain is slow now, a disposal adds more solids to a line that is already narrow, so clean the line before you install. We install across Duluth and in nearby Peachtree Corners, and permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office if you add new wiring.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) governs the trap and drain connection under the sink, and a disposal must be installed to those rules.

Using a new disposal properly

Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after. Feed small amounts at a time, and do not grind bones, fibrous peels, coffee grounds in bulk or pasta and rice that swell in the drain.

Clean the unit with ice and a little citrus peel when it smells. If it hums and will not turn, turn it off and see the jam section on our repair page.
