Sewer Cleanout in Duluth, GA: Where It Is and Why It Matters

The sewer cleanout is a capped pipe that gives plumbers direct access to the main line. Finding it before a backup saves time, and adding one can save money on every future service call.

What a sewer cleanout is

A sewer cleanout is a vertical pipe with a threaded cap that connects to the main sewer line. It usually sits flush with the ground or a few inches above it, and the cap is often plastic or cast iron. A plumber removes the cap to feed a cable or a camera straight into the line.

Without a cleanout, access means pulling a toilet or removing a vent cap, which is slower, costs more and risks damage. With one, a clog in the main line can be cleared in a fraction of the time.

Where to look for it

Cleanouts are placed for access, but the location varies.

Near the foundation

Look along the side or front of the house, near where the main line leaves.

In the front yard

Some homes have a cleanout partway between the house and the street.

At the property line

A cleanout near the street marks the end of the private lateral.

In the basement or crawlspace

Look on the floor or low on a wall, near the main drain.

In the garage

Some are set in the slab near an exterior wall.

Under a green or black lid

A plastic cap in the lawn may sit in a small box.

Signs you need a cleanout

Consider adding one if any of these apply.

  • 1Your house has no cleanout and the main line has clogged before.
  • 2The only access is a toilet or a roof vent.
  • 3Large trees grow near the sewer line.
  • 4You plan a camera inspection and the line has no entry point.
  • 5The existing cleanout is broken, buried or has a seized cap.

How a cleanout is installed

A plumber locates the line, digs to it and cuts in a fitting with a vertical riser and a cap. The riser is finished at or just above ground level so it can be reached later, and a cover protects it from the lawn mower.

It is a modest job compared with the savings. Our sewer line cleaning service uses cleanouts first, and a sewer inspection needs one to run the camera.

What the cap tells you

A cleanout cap that is tight and clean means the line is probably intact. A cap that is wet, stained or smells of sewage suggests that pressure is building behind it, and you should not open it. Call us and we will open it safely.

A cap that is cracked or missing lets in insects, water and dirt, and rainwater entering the line can overload it. Replacing a cap is cheap, so fix it early.

Cleanouts and camera inspections

A camera inspection runs through the cleanout, so a good one makes the footage faster to capture and more complete. Without access near the house, we have to pull a toilet and start from there, which can miss a problem near the foundation.

If you are buying a home, ask whether the house has an accessible cleanout. If it does not, plan to add one at closing, since the line cannot be inspected properly without it.

Lawn and landscaping tips

Place a small paver or a valve box cover around the cleanout and keep mulch away from the cap. Mark the spot with a landscape stake or a note in your records, and tell your lawn crew to avoid it. Leave room around it for a cable machine and a hose.

Records worth keeping

Write down where the cleanout is, what size it is and the date of any cleaning. Take a photo and keep it with your home papers. If you sell the house, the buyer's inspector may ask, and a clear record helps the sale go smoothly. If a plumber has run a camera, keep the footage too.

What to expect when we open a cleanout

We start by checking the cap, then open it slowly and watch for standing water. If water is present, the line is blocked past that point and the cable goes in. If it is dry, the blockage is likely closer to the house. Either way, the camera confirms the cause, and you see the footage on the monitor.

What to do if you cannot find a cleanout

A missing cleanout is a common situation in older homes.

  • 1Check the front yard between the house and the street.
  • 2Look along the foundation near the kitchen and bathrooms.
  • 3Check the basement, crawlspace and garage floor.
  • 4Look at your property survey or the plans from your closing.
  • 5Call us and we will locate the line with a camera and a tracer.

Why a cleanout adds value

A home with a cleanout is easier to maintain and to sell, and buyers with an inspector appreciate it. A cleanout makes every future service cheaper, and in an emergency it can cut the time to clear a backup from hours to minutes.

Cleanouts and sewer responsibility in Duluth

Gwinnett County Department of Water Resources operates the public sewer. According to the county's guidelines, the owner is responsible for the private lateral up to and including the connection to the public sewer. A cleanout near the property line is therefore a useful place to tell where your responsibility starts.

If a clog is in the public main in the street, the county handles it. A cleanout helps show which side the blockage is on. We serve the whole city and nearby Peachtree Corners, and permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office about new work.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for accessible cleanouts on building sewers, which is why a modern installation includes one.

Keep the cleanout accessible

Do not bury it under mulch or a new patio. Keep the cap clear, mark its place and make sure it is not damaged by mowing. If a cap will not turn, do not force it, since a broken cap can let sewage out. Call us and we will free it.
