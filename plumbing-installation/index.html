What plumbing installation covers

Installation means adding or replacing something in the plumbing system. That could be a new sink, a toilet, a shower valve, a washing machine hookup, an outdoor spigot or a new bathroom. Each job touches supply lines, drains and vents in its own way.

We start by looking at what is there and what the new fixture needs. The supply line, the drain size, the vent and the shutoff valve must all suit the fixture. We explain the plan and the price before we begin.

If the work is a repair rather than a new installation, our service pages cover faucet repair, toilet repair and garbage disposal repair.

Codes, permits and quality for Duluth installations

Georgia plumbing work follows the state plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments. Fixtures need proper venting, correct trap sizes and approved materials, and many projects need permits and inspection.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office what applies to your project. We pull permits where they are required. Georgia licenses plumbers through the Secretary of State's plumbers division, and you can verify any plumber's license on the state's lookup tool before you hire.

Choosing fixtures that last

Fixtures differ widely in quality. Solid brass valves and ceramic cartridges outlast plastic internals, and a faucet with a long warranty usually has parts that can be bought years later. Ask for the model number and check that parts are available before you buy.

Look for the WaterSense label on faucets, showers and toilets. It marks products that meet the EPA's water-efficiency and performance criteria, and it can reduce water use without losing flow where it counts.

Bring us the fixture or the model number before you order and we will tell you what it needs from the plumbing. Some wall-hung toilets, freestanding tubs and multi-head showers need extra framing or larger supply lines, and planning for that avoids surprises.

Codes and standards behind the work

New fixtures and lines must follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets rules for supply sizing, drains, vents and traps. The fixtures themselves should meet the standards listed on their labels, and permit rules vary, so we confirm what applies to your project.
