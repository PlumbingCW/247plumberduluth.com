What installation covers beyond swapping a tank

A swap of one tank for the same type is a replacement, and we cover it on the water heater replacement page. An installation is different. It means putting a heater where one did not exist, moving it, changing the fuel, or adding an on-demand unit, and each of those changes the pipes, vent and power around it.

We start with a walkthrough of the planned space. We measure clearances, check the gas line size and the electrical panel, locate the nearest drain and plan the vent path. That visit decides the type of unit that fits, and the price follows.

Planned installs are booked on a schedule that suits you. If a failed tank is forcing the decision today, call our 24/7 line and we will talk through the fastest option.

How a planned installation runs

Each step matters, and we explain them before work begins so the day has no surprises.

  • 1Site check. We confirm space, access, gas and electrical capacity, and local permit needs.
  • 2Unit selection. We match capacity and fuel type to your household and your budget.
  • 3Rough-in. Gas piping, vent, power and water lines are run or adjusted to the new location.
  • 4Set and connect. The unit goes on a pan or stand, with new valves, flexible lines and a discharge pipe.
  • 5Test and start-up. We purge air, check for leaks, test the relief valve and set the temperature.
  • 6Walkthrough. You learn the controls, the shutoffs and the yearly maintenance.

Code, permits and safe installation

Georgia plumbing work follows the state's plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments. A new installation may involve a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, a pan under heaters above finished space, a thermal expansion tank on a closed system, and a vent sized for the appliance.

Permit and inspection rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office, or Gwinnett County for unincorporated addresses, what applies to your project. We pull permits where they are required and will tell you before the work starts.

Gas appliances need correct combustion air and a sound flue. We test for gas leaks at every connection, and we check for carbon monoxide risks after start-up. If a gas smell appears at any time, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility.

Installations around Duluth

Newer Gwinnett subdivisions often put the water heater in a garage or an attic closet, while older Duluth houses may have it in a basement or a utility room. Each location affects drain access, venting and the damage a leak can cause.

A garage unit needs protection from impact and a pan with a drain. An attic unit needs a drain line that reaches a safe point, because a leak above a finished ceiling does quiet and costly damage. We plan for both. We install across Duluth, from the neighborhoods near Pleasant Hill Road to the newer streets toward the Chattahoochee River, and in Peachtree Corners.

Codes and standards behind the work

New installations are governed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the manufacturer's instructions and, for gas appliances, the fuel gas code adopted in Georgia. Those documents set clearances, venting, gas piping and relief valve requirements, and we work to them.
