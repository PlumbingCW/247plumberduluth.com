How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Duluth, GA

When a pipe bursts, the minutes before you find the shutoff cost money. Learn where the main valve usually sits, how to test it and what to do if it is stuck or missing.

Two shutoffs, two owners

Most homes have two places to stop the water. The first is the valve at the meter, in a box in the yard or at the curb. In Gwinnett County that valve and the meter belong to Gwinnett County Water Resources, and customers should not operate them without guidance.

The second is the main shutoff valve inside or just outside the house, where the line enters the building. This one is yours, and it is the valve to use in an emergency. Everyone in the house should know where it is and how it turns.

Where to look for the main valve

Valves are often placed where the line enters, but the location varies with the house.

Garage

Look on the wall that faces the street, often near the water heater.

Utility room or laundry

A valve may be near the washer or the heater.

Basement or crawlspace

Check along the front foundation wall, near where the line enters.

Exterior wall

Some homes have a valve on the outside wall in a small box.

Under the kitchen sink

This is a fixture valve and not the main, but it can stop one leak.

Near a pressure reducing valve

A bell-shaped valve on the line is often right next to the main.

Testing your valve

Test it now, not on the day of a leak.

  • 1Identify the type. A round handle is a gate valve and turns many times. A lever handle is a ball valve and turns a quarter turn.
  • 2Turn it slowly. Turn a gate valve clockwise until it stops. A ball valve is closed when the handle is crosswise to the pipe.
  • 3Open a faucet. Confirm the water stops at a faucet, then open the valve again.
  • 4Label the valve. Tag it so others can find it in a hurry.
  • 5Check for drips. Look for weeping at the stem and call us if you see it.
  • 6Exercise it yearly. Turning it once a year keeps it from seizing.

If the valve is stuck or leaking

Do not force a seized gate valve, since the stem can snap. Old gate valves are known for failing, and many plumbers recommend replacing them with a full-port ball valve. We can do that in under an hour in many homes.

If you cannot stop the water at all, ask the county about the meter valve, and call us. For leaks, our leak detection page explains finding them, and water line repair covers the line itself.

Gate valve or ball valve

Gate valves have a round wheel handle and a wedge inside that rises and falls as you turn. They are common in older homes and wear out because the wedge corrodes and sticks open. Ball valves have a straight lever and a ball with a hole, and they turn a quarter turn from open to closed.

A ball valve is more reliable. If your main is a gate valve that is stiff or weeping at the stem, replacing it with a full-port ball valve is a sensible upgrade. It takes a short time and may be done during a visit for another reason.

Fixture shutoffs help too

Each sink, toilet and appliance has its own small shutoff, so a leak at one fixture does not need the whole house to lose water. Learn where they are, and turn each one a few times a year so it does not freeze in place.

Replace corroded or leaking shutoff valves when you see them. New quarter-turn valves and braided supply lines cost little compared to the damage from one failed part.

Teach everyone in the house

Show every adult and older child where the main valve is and how it turns. Put a tag on it and a note in the kitchen. If you rent out a property, tell your tenants and keep a wrench near the valve if it needs one.

A dry run takes five minutes and can prevent a flood. Do it on a calm day, and check for weeping at the valve while you are there.

A five-minute emergency drill

Practice once so you can do it in the dark.

  • 1Locate the main valve and check that the path to it is clear.
  • 2Turn it closed, open a faucet to confirm and open it again.
  • 3Find the shutoff for the water heater and the washer.
  • 4Write the plumber's number on a card at the valve.
  • 5Take a photo of the valve and keep it with your home records.

Why valve upgrades pay off

A valve that works is a cheap piece of insurance. A flood from a failed fitting can cost thousands in repairs, and a quick shutoff reduces the damage. If your valve is stiff, leaks at the stem or is a gate type, replacing it is worth considering.

Shutoffs and meters in Duluth

Gwinnett County Water Resources owns the meter and the line to it. The owner is responsible for the pipe on the house side. Before you call the county, confirm the leak is not on your side, since the county will only repair its part.

Many Duluth homes have the main valve in the garage or a basement utility area. If you cannot find yours, send us a photo of where the line seems to enter and we will help. We serve all of Duluth and nearby Peachtree Corners.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires an accessible shutoff valve on the water service, so every Georgia home should have one that you can reach.

After you shut it off

Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure, and open one upstairs to let air in. Turn off the water heater if the water will be off for long, because an electric element can burn out when the tank drains.
