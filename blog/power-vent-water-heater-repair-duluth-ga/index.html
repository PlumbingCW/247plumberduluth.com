Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Duluth, GA

A power vent water heater uses a blower to push exhaust out through a plastic pipe. That blower and its safety switches are where most problems start, and some simple checks can save a service call.

How a power vent heater works

A power vent water heater has an electric blower mounted on the tank. When the thermostat calls for heat, the blower starts and pushes exhaust gases out through a plastic vent pipe, which can run horizontally through a wall or up through the roof.

A pressure switch confirms that the blower is moving air before the burner lights. If the vent is blocked or the blower fails, the switch keeps the burner off. This is a safety feature, and it is the reason these heaters show error codes or blink lights when something is wrong.

Power vent units need both gas and a power outlet, so a power outage or a tripped breaker leaves you without hot water.

Common power vent problems

Most calls trace to one of these causes.

No hot water and blower silent

The outlet has no power, the blower motor has failed or a wire has loosened.

Blower runs, burner will not light

The pressure switch, the vent or the gas valve may be at fault.

Error code or blinking light

The code points to the failing part. The manual lists them.

Burner lights then goes out

A dirty flame sensor or a weak draft shuts the burner off.

Loud blower noise

Worn bearings or debris in the blower wheel cause it.

Water pooling under the unit

A tank leak means replacement, and a condensate or relief valve leak is a smaller fix.

Safe checks before you call

These checks take a few minutes and need no tools.

  • 1Confirm the outlet has power and the breaker has not tripped.
  • 2Look at the vent pipe outside for bird nests, leaves or snow, and clear anything blocking the opening if you can do so safely.
  • 3Read the blinking light code on the control and look it up in the manual.
  • 4Check that the gas supply valve to the heater is open.
  • 5If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility. Do not use switches or phones near the smell.

What a repair involves

A technician tests the blower, the pressure switch and the thermostat, cleans the flame sensor and checks the vent for restrictions. Common repairs include a new blower motor, a new pressure switch, a new igniter or a new gas control valve.

A heater that is more than ten or twelve years old and needs a costly part may be better replaced. See water heater repair for the options, and water heater installation when a new unit or a new vent location is needed.

Why the vent and the blower matter

The blower pushes exhaust through a small-diameter pipe, and it has to move air against resistance. A long or crooked vent, or one with lint or nests inside, cuts airflow and trips the pressure switch. The burner then stays off for safety, even though the heater is otherwise fine.

Check the termination outside for a screen that is clogged or a cover that has loosened. A poorly sealed vent joint can also leak exhaust gas, which is a safety hazard, so any sign of soot or a smell around the heater needs an immediate visit.

Carbon monoxide and safety

Gas appliances can produce carbon monoxide if the vent fails or the burner runs poorly. A working carbon monoxide detector near the sleeping areas is the best protection. If it sounds, leave the house and call 911.

After any gas water heater repair, we check the burner flame and the vent joints. Do not ignore a heater that has soot on or around it.

Maintenance that prevents power vent problems

Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes and chemicals, and vacuum dust from the blower inlet each year. Check the outside vent termination after storms, flush the tank annually and test your carbon monoxide detector. These small habits catch most problems early, and they keep the blower and the pressure switch working as designed.

When a power vent heater should be replaced

A repair is not always the best use of money.

  • 1The tank leaks from the body.
  • 2The tank is more than 10 to 12 years old and needs a costly part.
  • 3The heater has repeated failures within a year.
  • 4The vent needs redoing because it no longer meets current rules.
  • 5Parts for the model are no longer sold.

Questions to ask the technician

Ask what failed and whether it can fail again. Ask whether the vent was tested, what the repair is guaranteed for and what the next likely failure would be. A plain answer is a good sign.

Power vent heaters in Duluth homes

Newer Gwinnett County subdivisions often put a power vent unit in the garage because it can vent through a side wall and does not need a chimney. These units are common in homes built since the late 1990s, and they rely on a clear vent termination outside.

Georgia plumbing and fuel gas work follows state codes, and permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office about replacement or relocation. We serve the whole city and nearby Peachtree Corners.

Gas water heaters are built to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard and installed under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the fuel gas code, which set the vent rules a technician checks.
