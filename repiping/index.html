Signs your pipes need replacing

A single leak is a repair. Several leaks in a short time, brown or rust-colored water, low pressure that gets worse and visible corrosion on pipes all point to a system that is wearing out. Each patch buys time, and each leak risks damage to walls and floors.

Older galvanized steel is the usual culprit. It corrodes on the inside, narrows and eventually leaks. Some older copper and early plastic piping materials have their own failure patterns, and our plumber checks which type you have before advising.

Repiping is a planned project, not an emergency fix. If a pipe is leaking today, we stop it first with burst pipe repair and then discuss the longer plan.

How a repipe is done

A clear plan reduces disruption.

  • 1Inspection. We trace the existing lines, test pressure and look for leaks.
  • 2Plan and price. You approve the route, the material and the price.
  • 3Rough-in. New lines are run through walls, attics or crawlspaces.
  • 4Connect fixtures. Each fixture and the water heater are tied into the new system.
  • 5Test. The system is pressure tested and checked for leaks.
  • 6Patch and clean. Access openings are patched and the work area is cleaned.

Repiping in Duluth homes

Many Duluth houses date from the 1980s and 1990s. Pipe materials in homes of that age vary, and we have seen galvanized supply lines, copper and early plastic systems around the area. A pressure test and a visual check will tell you what yours has.

Repiping a whole house usually takes a few days, and water is shut off during part of each day. We plan around your schedule. We serve all of Duluth, ZIP codes 30095 through 30099, and nearby Peachtree Corners. If the main line to the house is the weak point, see water line repair.

Permits, warranties and insurance

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Duluth permits and inspections office what applies. We pull permits where they are required and arrange the inspection.

Pipe manufacturers publish warranties for their products, and we give you the paperwork for the materials we install. Some insurers look favorably on repiped homes, so ask yours whether it affects your policy.

Preparing your home for a repipe

A repipe goes more smoothly when the work areas are clear. Move items away from walls near bathrooms and the kitchen, and clear a path to the attic hatch, the crawlspace door and the water heater. Cover or move furniture that cannot be taken out of the way.

Plan for water to be off for part of each workday, and fill a few containers for drinking and flushing. We schedule the shutoffs for the shortest windows we can and we restore service each evening when possible.

Dust and noise are part of the job, and we use drop cloths, barriers and a vacuum to keep them contained. After the repipe, we walk through the house with you to show the new valves, the shutoffs and any patches that need paint.

Codes and standards behind the work

Repiping follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC). PEX tubing is made to the ASTM F876 standard, and copper to ASTM B88. We use materials marked to those standards and follow the manufacturer's guidelines for support, bends and connections.
